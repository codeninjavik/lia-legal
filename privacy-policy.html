Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026 · Applies to LIA for Android, both editions

LIA ("LIA", "the app", "we", "us") is made by codeninjavik ("we", "our"). This policy explains what LIA actually does with your data - written to match the app's real code, not a generic template. LIA ships in two editions with different capabilities; where they differ, both are described explicitly.

In one paragraph: LIA sends what you say to Google's Gemini Live API, and what you type in Chat to Groq's API (or, only as a fallback, to Google's Gemini API), so it can reply - that's the core of how it works. Your voice and chat conversations are saved as text in a History list on your phone only. It reads your contacts only on your phone, to turn a name into a number, and never uploads your contact list. The full edition can optionally read and act on your screen when you ask it to, and can check a purchased access key against our database - the Play edition does neither. LIA now needs a free account (email or Google) so you can use voice rooms: live group audio with friends. That account keeps your name, a username and your photo on our server, and room audio is passed live to the people in the room (it is not recorded by us). LIA never sells your data, and never reads your screen or listens in the background unless you've explicitly turned that on yourself.

2. What LIA collects, and why

Voice audio

While a voice conversation is active, your microphone audio is streamed to Google's Gemini Live API in real time so LIA can understand and reply to you. Audio is not saved to a file on your device or on our servers. A short text recap of the recent conversation is kept in memory only, to keep context if the connection briefly reconnects - it is cleared when the session ends. The text transcript of the conversation (what you said and what LIA replied, as transcribed by the Gemini service) is saved to History on your device - see "Conversation history" below.

Typed chat messages

Text you type in Chat is sent to Groq (api.groq.com), an AI inference provider, as a standard one-off text request, separate from the voice connection, to generate a reply. The recent part of the conversation (up to the last 20 messages) is sent along with each new message so LIA remembers earlier turns. If Groq can't answer and you have saved a Gemini API key, the same request is sent to Google's Gemini API instead. Your chat text is not sent anywhere else.

Conversation history

LIA saves your typed chats and the text transcripts of your voice conversations in a History list so you can reopen them. This is stored only on your device, in LIA's private app storage (up to 200 conversations; the oldest are dropped first). It is not uploaded to any server we run and is excluded from Android's cloud backup. Voice audio is never saved. You can delete a single conversation by swiping it away in History, delete all of them with "Clear Conversation History" in Settings, or erase everything with "Clear all app data" or by uninstalling the app.

Website builder

If you ask LIA to build you a website (by voice or in Chat), your request is sent to Google's Gemini API using your Gemini API key to generate the page. The finished website is saved as a file in LIA's private storage on your device only; it is not uploaded to any server we run.

Contacts

If you ask LIA to call or message someone, it reads your device's Contacts (READ_CONTACTS) locally to match the name you said to a phone number. This lookup never leaves your device - LIA does not upload, transmit, or share your contact list with anyone, including Google, at any point.

Calls and messages

Calling and messaging are handed to your phone's own dialer or messaging app (SMS or WhatsApp) through a standard Android share/compose action. LIA does not intercept, store, or read your call or message history.

Installed apps

To open an app by name, LIA looks up your installed, launchable apps via Android's PackageManager. This list is used only on-device to match what you asked for; it is not uploaded anywhere.

Screen content — full edition only, optional

The full edition can optionally use Android's Accessibility service so LIA can see what's on screen and tap, type, or scroll for you when you ask it to - for example, posting a photo to Instagram. This is off by default and entirely optional; LIA works for voice, chat, opening apps, and calling/messaging without it. When it is on:

  • It reads visible text, button labels, and their positions in whichever app is currently open - only while actively carrying out something you asked for.
  • It does not run in the background and does not read your screen when you haven't asked it to do something.
  • Password fields are always excluded and never captured.
  • Nothing read this way is uploaded to our servers; it is used locally to find and tap the right button, then discarded.

See the full Accessibility Disclosure for details, or open it any time from LIA's Settings.

Media you share for posting — full edition only

If you share a photo or video to LIA (via Android's "Share" menu) so it can post it to Instagram or Facebook for you, LIA keeps a private copy on your device only, and asks you to confirm before keeping it. That copy is never uploaded to any server we run - it is only handed to the Instagram or Facebook app itself, through Android's normal share mechanism, once you post it (and LIA never publishes anything without your explicit "yes" first).

Access-key licensing — full edition only

The full edition checks a license key you enter against our database (Google Firestore, part of Firebase) so it knows whether to unlock the app. This check uses a device identifier derived from your phone's DRM module - not your name, email, or any personal account - plus the key itself. We use Firebase Authentication in "anonymous" mode only for this, which does not collect your email, phone number, or identity.

Your LIA account

LIA asks you to log in before you use it, with Google or with an email address and a password. Signing in is handled by Google Firebase Authentication. For voice rooms we then keep, on our server, your name, your username (you choose it right after you first log in, and can change it in Profile once a week; the old name becomes free for others), your profile photo address (from your Google account, if you use Google) and your phone's push-notification address (see below). Your email address and, for email accounts, your password (stored only as a one-way hash) live in Firebase Authentication; our room server does not store your email. Other people can find you by username, and see your name, username and photo in the rooms you are in. They never see your email or your account id: inside a room you appear under a random code.

Voice rooms, invitations and share links

When you join a voice room, your microphone audio is sent live to the other people in that room through LiveKit, a real-time audio service. LIA does not record room audio and keeps no copy of it; people in the room can hear you, and we cannot stop someone in the room from recording it on their own device - only join rooms with people you trust. We keep, per room: its name and description, who hosts it, who was invited and who sat in it (name, username, photo, time joined) and whether you muted yourself. A room's host can invite people, remove a person from the room, and end it for everyone. Anyone in the room can pass on the room's share link (Share button): anyone who opens the link while signed in is allowed to join that room, so share it only with people you want in. To report someone, tap their picture in the room and choose Report: it opens an email to us that contains the room and that person's room code. Rooms and everything attached to them (seats, invitations) are erased 30 days after the room ends.

Screen sharing in voice rooms

In a voice room you can choose to share your screen so the other people in that room can see it. This only starts when you tap Share screen and then accept Android's own "start recording or casting" prompt, which Android shows every time. While you share, Android and LIA show a notification, and sharing stops when you tap Stop sharing, leave the room, the host ends the room, or you swipe the notification's Stop.

  • Everything on your screen is visible to the people in the room while you share: other apps, messages, notifications, photos, names and numbers. LIA cannot black out private details for you (Android hides only the content of apps that mark themselves secure, such as many banking apps and password fields). Close or hide anything private before you start, and only share with people you trust.
  • The picture of your screen is sent live through LiveKit to the people in the room (and to the host). LIA does not record or store it, and keeps no copy. Other people in the room could still record what they see on their own device, and we cannot prevent that.
  • If you share your screen with sound, the sound LIA can capture from the phone is sent the same way. The room's host can remove you and stop your sharing at any time.
  • LIA does not look at, read, summarise or analyse what is on your screen when you share it; it only passes it on to the room.

Notifications about invitations

To tell you that someone invited you to a room, we store your phone's push address (a token from Google Firebase Cloud Messaging) next to your profile and send a short message (the room name and who invited you) through Google's push service. You can switch these notifications off in LIA (Broadcast & Voice Rooms) or in Android's notification settings. Logging out removes this phone's push address.

Voice keyboard

The LIA voice keyboard records what you say only while you are using it, and sends that audio to Groq (api.groq.com) using your own Groq key, to turn it into text (and, if you pick a translation mode, to translate it). The text is typed into the app you are using. LIA does not keep the audio or the text and does not read other things you type on any keyboard.

What we do not do

  • We do not use advertising or analytics SDKs of any kind in LIA.
  • We do not sell, rent, or trade your data to anyone, ever.
  • We do not upload your contacts list or read your screen for the account or for rooms.
  • We do not record voice-room audio or shared screens.
  • The Play edition does not use Accessibility and does not read your screen. It has no license check; it uses Firebase only for sign-in, voice-room invitations and push messages.

4. Where your data lives

Your LIA account profile, voice rooms and invitations are stored on our server (Google Firestore, reached through a Cloudflare Worker). Room audio is not stored anywhere. Ended rooms are erased after 30 days, invitations expire after 24 hours, and your profile stays until you delete your account (section 7).

Your preferences (assistant name, personality - including any custom personality text you write - language, voice, theme), your saved conversation History, and the media inbox for posting are stored locally on your device, and are excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup - see android:allowBackup="false" in the app - so they never leave your phone via backup. Your Gemini and Groq API keys, if you added them, are stored locally on your device only and are sent only to Google and Groq respectively, to authenticate your own requests. Full-edition license state (masked key, plan, expiry) is stored both on your device and in our Firestore database, tied to your device identifier, not your identity.

6. Children's privacy

LIA, its accounts and its voice rooms are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect data from children under that age. Voice rooms can put you in touch with people you do not know if you share a link or invite someone you only know by username: if you are under 18, use them only with people you trust. If you believe a child has used LIA and shared personal data with us, contact us at the address in §10 and we'll remove it.

7. Your choices and rights

Delete your account and data

In the app: open LIA → Profile → Delete account and confirm. This immediately erases, from our server, your profile (name, username, photo address, push address), your invitations, your seats in rooms and every room you host (they are ended for everyone first). Your username becomes free for others. Without the app: email codeninjavik@gmail.com from the address you signed in with, with the subject "Delete my LIA account", and we will do the same and also remove your sign-in record (email) from Firebase Authentication within 30 days. Log out (Profile → Log out) only signs you out of this phone; it keeps your account.

Other choices

  • Turn off invitation notifications in Broadcast & Voice Rooms or in Android's settings.
  • Leave any room at any time; the host can also remove you.
  • Turn off Accessibility any time from LIA's Settings (full edition) - the app keeps working for everything else.
  • Turn off "Auto Listening" in Settings if you don't want LIA to start listening automatically when you open the app.
  • Revoke permissions any time from Android's own App Info screen.
  • Delete your conversation History any time: swipe a conversation away in History, or use "Clear Conversation History" in Settings.
  • Delete everything LIA has stored on your device by uninstalling the app - since nothing is backed up to the cloud (see §4), this removes it completely from your device. A full-edition license key remains recorded against your device identifier in our database (so re-entering it later still works) unless you contact us to have it cleared.
  • Ask us to delete your license record by emailing us (§10).

8. Security

Our room server checks your signed-in identity on every request, hands out room-audio passes that expire quickly, limits how often an account can create rooms or invite people, and keeps its secrets (service keys) out of the app.

Traffic to Google's Gemini API, to Groq's API and to Firebase is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). We don't store your API keys or license data in plain, unencrypted form beyond what's described above. No method of transmission or storage is 100% secure, and we can't guarantee absolute security, but we don't take shortcuts with what we control.
