Accessibility Disclosure full edition only

This is a web copy of the exact disclosure LIA shows in-app (Settings → Accessibility Status) before you're ever sent to Android's system toggle.

This is optional. LIA works for talking, opening apps, and calling or messaging contacts without it - you'll just tap Send yourself on messages. Turn it on only if you also want LIA to act on screen for you, like posting a photo or filling in a form, when you ask it to by voice. This feature does not exist in the Google Play edition of LIA at all.

What it reads

The screen of whichever app is open

Visible text, button labels, and their on-screen positions in the app you're currently using - only while it's carrying out something you asked for. It does not read your screen in the background, and password fields are never captured.

What it can do

Tap, type, and scroll for you

Only in the app currently in front of you, and only in direct response to a request you made in that conversation - for example "Instagram par ye photo post karo". It never acts on its own, and never runs while you're not asking it to do something.

What it never does

  • No background monitoring - it's inactive except while actively carrying out a request you made.
  • No password capture - password fields are excluded from every screen reading.
  • No use outside what you asked - it doesn't act on other apps, settings, or accounts beyond the task you gave it.
  • No login/CAPTCHA/2FA bypass - LIA stops and hands control back to you the moment it sees one, rather than trying to get past it.

Turning it off

Open LIA → Settings → Accessibility Status → "Turn off in system settings", any time. LIA keeps working for voice, chat, opening apps, and calling/messaging without it.
